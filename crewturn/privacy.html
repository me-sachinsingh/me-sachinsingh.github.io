Crewturn Privacy Policy

Effective date: 10 November 2026

Publisher: Sachin Singh, individual developer, India. In this policy, "we" and "us" mean the publisher.

Contact: skydays.support@gmail.com. That address is also the support address for Sky Days, our other app, so please put "Crewturn" in your subject line.

Crewturn is a rotating shift calendar for Android, free with banner ads. It has no account and we run no server for it. We don't receive your schedule, notes, swap names or pay rates, and the app doesn't put them in ad requests. They're kept on your phone, and can leave it only in the ways listed under "Calendar copy" and "Backup and sharing". One of those, Android's own backup, may already be on through your phone's settings. The app also uses Google's ad software, which collects some data about your phone and how you use the app. This page explains both. See also the Terms of Use.

Your schedule is sensitive

A work schedule can show when you're away from home, so think about who can see yours. Any home-screen widget you add shows your shifts: the next-shift widget shows today's and the next one, and the week widget shows a shift word for each day. When an alarm rings, the shift name can show on your lock screen, on the ring screen and in its notification, and other alarm notifications can show it too, depending on your phone's lock-screen settings. A calendar you copy to, a backup file you save and a picture you share can all be seen by anyone with access to them. Backup files aren't encrypted by the app.

What the app keeps on your phone

Your shift pattern, days you changed, notes, names you enter for shift swaps and who owes whom a shift, pay rates, alarm settings, and your choices in Settings. The app also keeps short lists of upcoming shifts and alarms (shift names and times only) so widgets can draw and alarms can ring after a restart, and a short list of alarms that were missed or not stopped, so it can tell you. We can't see any of it.

What we hold ourselves

We hold very little, but not nothing.

  • Emails you send us: your address, your message and anything you attach.
  • Purchase records: if you buy Remove ads, Google Play gives us order reports with the order number, product, price, your country and, where available, your state and postcode. We don't get your card details.
  • Crash and performance reports: Google Play may show us reports about crashes and performance from phones whose owners allow Android to share diagnostics. They can include the phone model, Android version and technical logs.
  • Ad reports: AdMob shows us totals, such as ad views and earnings by country. We don't get your Advertising ID.
  • These web pages: they're hosted on GitHub, which logs visitors' IP addresses for security. The pages carry no ads or ad trackers.

Ads and Google AdMob

The free version shows a banner ad from Google AdMob, using the Google Mobile Ads SDK, on the Home and Hours screens only. The app starts Google's ad software when you finish setup. In the UK, it first shows the consent form described below. The first banner appears the next day; if you restore a backup, the app keeps that backup's first-use date, so banners can start sooner. Banners don't show when you open the app from an alarm, a notification or a widget, while an alarm is sounding, for 10 minutes after you stop or snooze an alarm, or once you own Remove ads. The ad request is made by Google's software, and your schedule, notes, swap names and pay rates are not part of it.

Google's disclosure says its SDK collects, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention:

  • your IP address, which Google may use to estimate your approximate location;
  • how you use the app and ads, such as app launches and taps;
  • diagnostic information, such as launch time, hang rate and energy use;
  • device or other IDs, including the Android Advertising ID and app set ID.

Google says it encrypts this data in transit. Google decides how it is used and kept, under Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses data from partner apps. Google's own description is at its AdMob data disclosure. We never receive your schedule, notes or pay rates, so we can't sell them. Ad data is different, and the section on US state rights explains how some state laws may treat it.

You can reset or delete your Advertising ID in your phone's settings (usually under Privacy, then Ads). Once you own Remove ads, the app stops requesting ads and, from your next launch, doesn't ask Google's ad software to start. That software stays installed with the app, and part of it may still load when the app opens. If you later get a refund, ads and the ad software come back.

Who is responsible for ad data

We chose to put Google's ad software in the app, and Google decides what happens to the data after it receives it. In the UK, we consider ourselves responsible together with Google for collecting that data and passing it to Google, and Google alone is responsible for what it does next. You can come to either of us. Google may work with other advertising companies, and the consent form lists them.

Consent and how to change it

In the UK, the app shows Google's consent form before it starts Google's ad software. If you say no, you won't get personalised ads, but the app may still start the ad software and show "limited ads". Limited ads don't use your data to personalise ads. Google still receives your IP address to deliver the ad, and it may store information on your phone to detect fraud. Google's consent tool has to run first to work out your region and save your answer, so it connects to Google before you choose. Until it has finished, no ads load, and if it can't finish, for example with no connection, it tries again next time.

You can change your answer at any time under Ad privacy choices in Settings. That option appears once the consent tool has run on a normal launch of the app. If you opened the app from an alarm, a notification or a widget, Settings may show a link to this page instead; open the app from its icon and the option is there, except while an alarm is sounding or for 10 minutes after you stop or snooze one, when the app shows no ads and doesn't ask. If you own Remove ads the app doesn't run the consent tool, and it requests no ads. Where consent isn't required, the app starts the ad software at the same points without a form, and Settings points to this page.

Purchases

Remove ads is a one-time purchase, sold by us through Google Play, which takes payment on our behalf. Google's privacy policy applies to the payment. We don't see your card details. The app asks Google Play whether your Google account owns the purchase. Google's billing software may log technical details about the purchase process.

Calendar copy (optional, off by default)

If you turn it on, the app asks for calendar permission at that moment, not before. It writes your shifts for the next four months into a phone calendar you pick. Each event holds only the shift's start and end times, time zone, a title such as "Night shift" or "12-hour shift", using whatever shift name you entered, and a marker so the app can recognise its own events. Notes, swap names and pay are never included. To let you pick, the app reads the names of the calendars on your phone and the accounts they belong to, on your phone only. The app only changes events it made itself. Turning it off, or using Delete all my data, tries to remove the events the app made. If it can't, for example because you turned off calendar permission, you can remove them yourself in your calendar app. If the calendar you chose syncs to an account (for example Google Calendar) or is shared with other people, those shifts then follow that calendar's own rules and its provider's privacy policy.

Backup and sharing

  • Backup file: you choose where it is saved, which may be a folder synced by a service such as Google Drive. The file holds your schedule data, including notes, pay rates and any names you entered for swaps, which are other people's names. It is not encrypted by the app. It stays under your control, and its location follows that service's rules. It does not include ad consent or purchase status. If you turn on Save automatically, the app rewrites that file when you open the app, about once a day.
  • Android Auto Backup: the app allows Android to back up its data files to your Google account only when the backup is end-to-end encrypted by your screen lock, and to carry them over in a direct transfer when you move to a new phone. That direct transfer doesn't have the encryption condition. These files hold your schedule data, notes, pay rates and swap names. The app's backup rules leave out its settings storage, which is where ad consent state is kept. You can turn Auto Backup off in your phone's settings.
  • Share image: the picture of your month is created only when you tap share, and goes where you send it. It holds no names, notes, pay or hours. The app deletes its temporary file when sharing finishes. The sharing tool keeps a copy in the app's cache until you next share, use Delete all my data, or Android clears the cache.

Alarms, notifications and permissions

  • Alarms and notifications: the app rings shift alarms and shows notifications, using exact alarms, a full-screen alarm screen over the lock screen, vibration, keeping the phone awake and a foreground service while an alarm rings. It restarts your alarms after a reboot, app update, or time or time zone change. All of this happens on your phone. The lists it needs for this are kept where Android can read them after a restart, before you unlock the phone, so that an alarm can still ring.
  • Calendar: read and write calendar permission, only when you turn on calendar copy.
  • Advertising ID, Android ad services, billing, internet and network state: used by Google's ad and purchase software as described above.

Deleting your data

Settings has Delete all my data. It removes your pattern, changed days, notes, swap names, pay rates, alarms, the widgets' list of shifts, alarm notices, any waiting snooze, shared images and your backup link from the phone. It also tries to remove the calendar events the app made; see "Calendar copy". Any widget you placed then asks you to open the app. Uninstalling the app also removes its data from the phone. Backup files you saved stay where you put them, and Android may keep a cloud backup until it is next updated. Data that Google holds from ads is subject to Google's controls, not ours. We can delete what we hold ourselves if you ask.

Why we can use this data (UK)

  • Ad software and ads: your consent, including for reading and storing information on your phone. You can withdraw it at any time.
  • Limited ads, if you decline: our legitimate interest in funding the free app with ads that, as Google describes them, don't use your data to personalise ads.
  • Support emails: our legitimate interest in answering you and keeping a record of what was said.
  • Remove ads: to perform our contract with you.
  • Purchase records: to meet our tax and accounting duties, and our legitimate interest in dealing with refunds and disputes.
  • Crash reports: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working.

We don't make decisions about you using only automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects. Google uses automated systems to choose which ad to show.

How long we keep it

  • Support emails: 24 months after our last contact with you, then deleted.
  • Purchase records: as long as tax law requires.
  • Crash and ad reports: as long as Google makes them available to us. We don't export them with anything that identifies you.
  • Data on your phone: until you delete it or uninstall the app.
  • Data held by Google from ads: Google sets the period. See Google's retention page.

How data is protected

The app keeps your data in Android's private storage for the app, protected by your phone's own security. The app adds no separate encryption, and backup files aren't encrypted. Google says its ad software encrypts data in transit using TLS. Our support email is a Gmail account, and only the publisher reads it.

Where data goes

We are in India, and we read support emails and purchase records there, so that data is handled outside the UK. When you email us, you send your data to us directly. Google processes data in the US and other countries. For UK data, Google's terms use the UK's standard contract clauses, which you can read here. Data you keep in the app doesn't leave your phone through us.

Your rights in the UK

You can ask us for a copy of your data, and ask us to correct it, delete it or restrict how we use it. You can object to our use based on legitimate interests, and you can always object to direct marketing. We don't send any. You can withdraw consent under Ad privacy choices in Settings. We'll reply within one month. We can't link data held by Google to you, because the app has no account and we never hold your Advertising ID, so for that data we'll help you use My Ad Center and your phone's ad settings. Data on your phone is yours to export or delete yourself.

Crewturn isn't offered in the EEA. If you use it while you're there, Google's consent form may appear and you have the same rights described for the UK.

Complaints

If you're unhappy with how we've used your data, email us with "Data complaint" in the subject line. We'll acknowledge it within 30 days and tell you the outcome without undue delay. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at any time.

Children

Crewturn is for adults aged 18 and over. It's not directed to children, and we don't knowingly collect their data. We have no way to point Google to a particular phone, so if you think a child has used the app, delete it from that phone and reset or delete the Advertising ID in the phone's settings. If you email us about this, we'll delete your email once we've answered and help however we can.
